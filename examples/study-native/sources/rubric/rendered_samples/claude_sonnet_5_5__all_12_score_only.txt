## Role and Context

You are an impartial assessor with expertise in analytic assessment of argumentative essays and writing by 9th grade secondary school second-language learners whose first language is German.

## Task

Assess the student essay in response to the writing task using the supplied analytic rubric. Score all 12 traits separately in rubric order. Base each score on that trait's descriptors rather than an overall impression of the essay.

Assign a score of 0, 1, or 2 using the rubric descriptors. When several descriptions are listed for score 1, any one of them is sufficient. Do not introduce additional scoring criteria.

## Writing Task

The following writing task was provided to students. The time limit was 15 minutes.

“The internet makes traveling unnecessary.”

What do you think?

Write an argumentative essay in English, in which you discuss the statement.

## Rubric

### 1. Introductory Sentence
Trait ID: `introductory_sentence`

**Score 2**

There is an introductory sentence (or several), which leads to the essay’s topic.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is an introductory sentence, but it does not align with the topic of the writing prompt.
- There is an introductory sentence, but it does not align with the rest of the essay.
- There is an introductory sentence, but it does not align with the topic or the rest of the essay.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 2. Thesis Statement
Trait ID: `thesis_statement`

**Score 2**

There is a clear thesis statement, which expresses the student's opinion about the topic.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is a thesis statement, but it does not align with the topic of the writing prompt.
- There is a thesis statement, but it does not align with the rest of the essay.
- An opinion is expressed, but it remains implicit what exactly the opinion refers to, i.e., the topic of the writing prompt.
- There is a thesis statement, but this is not placed in the introduction.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 3. Transitional Sentence
Trait ID: `transitional_sentence`

**Score 2**

There is a transitional sentence, which says what will follow in the main body.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is a transitional sentence, but it announces something different from what follows in the main body.
- There are fractions of a transitional sentence, but no specific mention of the argumentative moves following in the main body.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 4. Counterargument
Trait ID: `counterargument`

**Score 2**

There is a counterargument that opposes the pro-arguments.

**Score 1**

There are fractions of one or more counterarguments, but they lack validity, force, or specificity to properly challenge the initial thesis statement.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 5. Rebuttal
Trait ID: `rebuttal`

**Score 2**

For every counter-argument, there is a rebuttal, which challenges the counterargument.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is a fraction of a rebuttal, but it lacks validity, force, or specificity to properly refute the counterargument.
- There is not a suitable rebuttal for every counterargument.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 6. Pro-Arguments
Trait ID: `pro_arguments`

**Score 2**

There are at least two pro-arguments, which support the thesis statement.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is one pro-argument that supports the thesis statement, but a second one is missing.
- There are two (or more) pro-arguments supporting the thesis statement, but they are not placed in the main body of the essay.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 7. Support
Trait ID: `support`

**Score 2**

There is support for each pro-argument, such as an explanation, an example, an expert’s opinion, or facts.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- At least one of the pro-arguments lacks support.
- At least one of the pro-arguments has insufficient/inappropriate support.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 8. Summary Sentence
Trait ID: `summary_sentence`

**Score 2**

There is a summary of the main arguments, which briefly recaps the student's ideas.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There are fractions of a summary of the main arguments, but a majority of arguments are repeated verbatim.
- In addition to a summary of the main arguments, one or more new arguments are introduced.
- There is a fraction of a summary, which contains a verbatim repetition of the main arguments and, additionally, new arguments are introduced.
- There is a summary of the main arguments, but this is not placed at the beginning of the conclusion.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 9. Rephrasing of the Thesis Statement
Trait ID: `rephrasing_of_the_thesis_statement`

**Score 2**

There is a rephrasing of the thesis statement, which uses different words than in the introduction.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is no rephrasing of the initial thesis statement, instead a new thesis is presented in the conclusion.
- The initial thesis statement is repeated verbatim.
- There is a rephrasing of the thesis statement, but this is not placed in the conclusion.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 10. Closing Sentence
Trait ID: `closing_sentence`

**Score 2**

There is a closing sentence, such as a call to action, a piece of advice, a prediction, or a compromise.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- There is a fraction of a closing sentence (e.g., call to action, advice, prediction, compromise), but it does not align with the rest of the essay.
- The closing sentence (e.g., call to action, advice, prediction, compromise) is not placed at the end of the essay.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 11. Paragraph Breaks
Trait ID: `paragraph_breaks`

**Score 2**

There are paragraph breaks, after the introduction, (when a new idea starts), and after the main body.

**Score 1**

Any one of the following:
- Not all necessary paragraph breaks (after the introduction, after the main body) are inserted.
- Some or all necessary paragraph breaks (after the introduction, after the main body) are inserted, but incorrect paragraph breaks (within the introduction or the conclusion) are also inserted.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

### 12. Linking Words
Trait ID: `linking_words`

**Score 2**

There are linking words or phrases, which meaningfully connect the student's ideas.

**Score 1**

There are linking words or phrases but they are used only partially or with partial correctness.

**Score 0**

Criterion not yet fulfilled.

## Student Essay

Some people say that the internet makes traveling unnecessary, but I disagree. I think visiting places is important because we can learn new things and meet other people.

First, when we travel we can see places with our own eyes. Pictures on the internet are useful, but they cannot show how a place feels. For example, at a museum we can look closely at old objects and ask questions. Second, traveling helps us talk with people from different cultures. We can practice languages and learn about their daily lives.

Some people argue that online tours are cheaper and easier than travel. This may be true, but watching a video is different from having a real conversation or walking around a new city.

In conclusion, traveling teaches us through experiences and conversations. Even though the internet provides information, it cannot fully replace visiting other places. We should use the internet to plan trips, not instead of traveling.
